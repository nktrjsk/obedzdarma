# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Czech-speaking general readers — ordinary people who encounter "free" offers (free apps, free services, free money, cheap goods, free schooling) and take them at face value. They arrive curious or skeptical, often via a share link, and read on a phone or laptop in a single sitting. They are not economists; the job they want done is to *understand, in plain language, what they're actually paying* — and to come away with a sharper instinct for spotting the hidden cost next time.

## Product Purpose

obedzdarma.cz ("a free lunch") is a single long-scroll persuasion page built on one thesis: **there is no such thing as a free lunch.** It walks the reader through four "currencies" you really pay with when something looks free — Soukromí (privacy), Efektivita (efficiency), Životní prostředí (environment), Inflace (inflation) — each anchored by a real, cited fact and concrete everyday examples. Success is a reader who finishes convinced, can name the four currencies, and trusts the argument enough to share it. The page must persuade without ever sacrificing factual credibility.

## Brand Personality

Sharp, modern, editorial — a crisp opinion piece, not a textbook and not a rant. Confident and provocative in its claims, but earns the provocation with sourced facts. Voice is informal Czech *ty* in written-standard register (never spoken-colloquial slang). Three words: **incisive, credible, unhedged.** It states the uncomfortable conclusion plainly and lets the evidence carry it.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS landing** — gradient hero, identical feature-card grid, rounded icons above every heading, tracked-uppercase eyebrows. The page argues; it does not "convert."
- **Cream / editorial-magazine aesthetic** — warm-paper background, display-serif italics, drop caps, broadsheet grid. "Editorial" describes the *voice*, never this saturated visual lane.
- **Corporate / neutral** — safe muted slate-and-beige, conflict-averse hedging. The opposite of a committed argument.
- **Conspiracy / clickbait** — despite being provocative, it must never read as tinfoil-hat or rage-bait. Every strong claim stays credible and sourced.

## Design Principles

1. **The argument never backs down to sound mainstream.** Claims are judged by mechanism and evidence, not by whether they sound conventional. Provocation is allowed; hedging to seem safe is not.
2. **Every strong claim is anchored to a real, citable source.** Persuasive simplification is fine; misleading omission is not. Numbered sources are part of the design, not a footnote afterthought.
3. **Provocation earns its keep with credibility.** Bold conclusions are paired with verifiable facts so the page reads as confident, not unhinged.
4. **Native scroll, always.** No scroll-hijacking, ever. The reading experience is the product; the reader stays in control.
5. **Voice over decoration.** Typography, copy, and pacing carry the persuasion. Imagery and motion serve the argument; they never become the point.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target **WCAG 2.2 AA.** Body text ≥4.5:1 contrast against its background, large text ≥3:1. Every animation has a `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` alternative. Semantic structure (headings, `<figure>`/`<figcaption>`, `<ol>` for sources), keyboard-navigable, and fully readable without JavaScript — the static page is the canonical reading experience.
